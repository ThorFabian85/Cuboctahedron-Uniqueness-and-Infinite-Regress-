A philosophical proposal with conditional mathematical results.

01 / The proposal

A foundation without
a first moment.

Ask what caused something, then what caused its cause. The question can continue indefinitely. Pettersen’s proposal asks whether reality’s foundation could remove the need for that chain altogether.

His starting commitment is that absolute nonexistence is a fabrication of language. For this investigation, the necessity of existence is treated as an axiom. A further commitment rules out a vicious foundational regress: existence cannot depend on an explanation that is perpetually deferred.

The proposed answer is timeless balance. Here, “balance” means complete explanatory closure. The foundation has no external cause, no earlier moment that switches it on, and no fundamental expansion into an already existing space.

“Reality has this form because every genuinely different form would fail to make existence coherent.”

The necessity claim endorsed by Pettersen

One activity, two inseparable aspects.

In the model, every expansion is met by contraction with exactly zero delay. The dual torus names one integrated system of complementary activity. It is intended to express a reality that remains zero-dimensional while giving rise to the universe we experience.

The unfolded cuboctahedron represents timeless equilibrium; its folding and unfolding represent the activity associated with spacetime. The animal mind models that underlying activity as experienced space, objects, and time. Mind belongs to the foundation rather than arriving as an external observer.

These statements describe the philosophical model. The investigation below asks how much of that account can currently be expressed and demonstrated mathematically.

02 / What we established

From intuition
to explicit conditions.

The collaboration turned a broad proposal into several narrower statements that can be examined independently.

IExact identity

Opposition can be specified at every phase.

If two contributions satisfy b(p) = −a(p), their sum is zero for every phase label p. This captures simultaneous cancellation without a waiting interval in the equation. The label need not be a physical clock.

a(p) + b(p) = 0

What it establishes: exact cancellation. Identifying those contributions with a unique dual-torus geometry, or with zero physical extent, requires additional arguments.

IIConditional construction

An unchanged whole can contain structured relations.

In a finite-dimensional mathematical construction, a paired state remains unchanged when one part undergoes a unitary transformation and the other undergoes its complex conjugate. Internal conditional descriptions can still display ordered relationships.

This gives a precise example of a globally invariant whole with internal structure. It does not establish that the whole is our universe, that its relations are conscious, or that ordered dependence has been explained away.

The mathematical statement +

Choose a dimension d, paired bases, and the normalized state

|Ωd⟩ = (1/√d) ∑j |j⟩ ⊗ |j̄⟩.

For every unitary U on that space,

(U ⊗ Ū)|Ωd⟩ = |Ωd⟩.

For the chosen dimension and pairing, the pure invariant state is unique up to an overall phase. Those choices are assumptions of the construction; the invariance does not select them. The broader idea of relational time has an established literature.3

IIIConditional theorem

Four geometric assumptions select the cuboctahedron.

The strongest uniqueness result applies to a finite set of equal vectors with specified reflection properties. Under all four assumptions below, the cuboctahedron is the only possibility, up to rotation and scale.

This is an application of established root-system mathematics to the proposal. It is the clearest completed geometric step in the investigation.1, 2

03 / The geometric result

A unique answer.
Under stated rules.

The proof succeeds within a precise mathematical setting. Its assumptions are part of the result.

Read the full demonstration ↗
  1. 01

    Three dimensions, equal length. The nonzero vectors span a three-dimensional Euclidean space and all have the same length.

  2. 02

    Reflection closure. Reflection in the plane perpendicular to any member preserves the entire vector set.

  3. 03

    One irreducible collection. The set cannot split into two independent, mutually perpendicular collections.

  4. 04

    Integer reflection coefficients. For every pair of vectors, 2(u · v)/(v · v) is an integer.

Therefore

The root system A₃ ≅ D₃

(±1, ±1, 0)   (±1, 0, ±1)   (0, ±1, ±1)

Twelve vectors. Their endpoints form a cuboctahedron.

Follow the short proof +

Root-system theory supplies three independent simple roots. Normalize their squared lengths to 2. Distinct simple roots have inner products 0 or −1. Connect two roots when their inner product is −1.

Irreducibility requires a connected graph. With three vertices, this is a path or a triangle. The triangle gives a Gram matrix with diagonal 2 and every off-diagonal entry −1. Its determinant is zero, contradicting independence.

Only the path remains: A₃. The twelve coordinates above realize that root system and form the cuboctahedron.1 Direct coordinate checks also give ∑v = 0 and ∑vvᵀ = 8I: cancellation and an equal second-moment distribution in every direction.

The central distinction

A condition can select a structure without yet explaining why reality must satisfy that condition.

Where the argument
still needs a bridge.

The open implication is between complete explanatory closure and the four geometric assumptions. We have not demonstrated that excluding a vicious regress forces precisely those rules.

Cuboctahedron

The integer condition matters. If it is removed, the H₃ root system provides another equal-length, irreducible, reflection-preserving collection: thirty vectors whose endpoints form an icosidodecahedron.2 Its vectors also cancel and have an equal second-moment distribution.

This does not establish H₃ as an alternative explanation of reality. It shows why the translation from philosophical balance to a particular set of geometric rules must be justified.

04 / What remains

The next work
is specific.

The task is to connect the completed mathematical steps to the philosophical necessity claim.

  1. 01

    Define what it means to close regress.

    State an independent criterion for explanatory closure, including which dependencies are inadmissible and how a proposed foundation satisfies the criterion.

  2. 02

    Derive the geometric conditions.

    Show that closure requires each of the four assumptions—especially three-dimensional representation and the integer reflection condition—rather than selecting them to obtain the desired geometry.

  3. 03

    Connect the cuboctahedron and dual torus.

    Specify the folding, flow, and correspondence mathematically. Demonstrate which features are preserved and why the required dynamics are unique.

  4. 04

    Establish the nonspatial interpretation.

    Show how exact opposition entails the intended zero-dimensional, timeless foundation, including what replaces temporal succession and how internal dependence is resolved.

  5. 05

    Connect the foundation to experience.

    Account for mind, distinctions, and experienced spacetime within the same system. A physical account would also need to recover observations and identify ways it could be tested.

Where we stand

A philosophical conclusion.
A formal argument in progress.

For Pettersen, balance resolves regress philosophically and requires the dual torus. The mathematical investigation has established several conditional results, but has not yet proved that this is the only coherent form of reality.

There is no defensible percentage for how close the full proof is. The remaining implications are substantive research questions.
